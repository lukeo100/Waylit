# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and the project aims to follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/) once it reaches a first release.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Live phone GPS and free ride (Amendment 8): a Position choice in the settings sheet (Simulator or Phone GPS) and `?gps=sim|live|replay`, the simulator staying the default. `LiveGpsSource` in `@waylit/core` wraps `watchPosition` with accuracy filtering (dropped above 50 m, weak above 25 m), low pass speed and heading, position smoothing, stale detection and plain statuses for denied, unavailable, weak and lost. A free ride has no route: the HUD shows GPS speed, distance and time, the minimap shows only the rider, and the scene shows only the rider and heading marker. The permission is asked only on the Start tap, after an explanation, and the hold to press guard uses the GPS speed. Daylight uses the real position and time. `?gps=replay` runs a generated trace through the same path with no location. A ground height estimator uses the phone altitude (smoothed, clamped, with a settings fallback and `?ground=` winning) and never reads tiles. No location is stored, logged or sent (`docs/PRIVACY.md`). Tests for the source, replay, ground estimator, settings, notices and free ride session. Not verified on a real phone yet.

- HUD redesign tools: `HudFrame` (the riding screen layout without data, shared by the ride page and the dev preview), `?hud=1` on `/dev/holo-preview` to draw the real HUD with fixed mock values over the procedural scene (no Google tiles, no network), `readability.ts` with the plate opacity and the WCAG maths, and new original screenshots `docs/images/hud-preview-*.png`.
- Tests: `readability.test.ts` (every text colour over the scene on its plate, at five daylight levels, against pure white, pure black, the day and night palettes, a sunlit photographic ground and the hologram cyan, plus the dock text on its glass) and `no-screen-darkening.test.ts`, which fails if a scrim, a screen wide gradient, a full screen fill, a vignette, a scrim token or a wide text shadow comes back. New render tests for the plates, the frame and the turn arrow. The layout check now also starts a trip and checks the riding screen for overlaps, for any element painting more than 20 percent of the screen or a band across it, and for WCAG AA of the text on its plate from the browser's computed colours.

- Map switch (Amendment 7): a round 44 pixel button beside the minimap opens a settings sheet with the Map (Google 3D tiles or Fallback map) and the Hologram screen effect (Off, Low, High). The choice is saved in localStorage and the address (`?map=`, `?fx=`) still wins and shows as locked. Switching the map starts the other provider through the same selection and one-shot fallback rules. The effect level changes on a running Google scene in place, so it costs no Cesium ion request. The sheet notes the Cesium ion quota (about 1,000 root requests per month on the Community plan), disables Google with a plain sentence when the build has no token, needs a hold above 8 km/h like the other controls and closes by itself when the guard turns on. The layout check covers the button size and the open sheet. Effect stays off by default on Google tiles.
- Holographic route guidance (task 9, awaiting Amendment 7): a cyan hologram look for the 3D scene on both providers behind the `MapProvider` interface (new `setRoute` and `setGuidance`). A translucent route ribbon with brighter rails on the road, floating chevrons that follow the route and bend toward the next turn, a vertical turn beacon with a distance countdown and the turn direction (a DOM label inside the scene, never over the credits strip), and a holographic rider on an original stylised kick scooter with a scanline look and a heading ring and cone. `RouteGuide` in `@waylit/map-provider` builds the corridor and the per frame guidance from the demo route and the simulated GPS.
- Turn instruction logic in `@waylit/core` (`nextTurn`, `turnPhase`, `directionOf`): the next real turn, its distance in the same rounding as the HUD and a phase (far, approaching, imminent, now). Tested against the `GpsSimulator` countdown, which satisfies brief success criterion 4.
- Google mode: the corridor is a `ClassificationPrimitive` (GPU stencil, `CESIUM_3D_TILE` only) at the documented ground height constant, and the chevrons, beacon, rider and heading marker are flat translucent primitives with original shaders. No height, depth, pick or terrain call touches the tiles, and a test guards the Google mode files for those APIs. The hologram screen effect (scan band, and a screen space ground grid at `high`) extends the existing fx post process and stays off by default for Google tiles.
- MapLibre fallback: the full look with no Google restrictions. Dark glass buildings with floor lines, roof edges and glowing outlines (extruded style layers in non-overlapping height bands), ribbon and glowing rails style layers, a world fixed ground grid, and one custom WebGL layer for the rider, chevrons, beacon and heading marker with the same original GLSL.
- Dev page `/dev/holo-preview?dev=1&engine=cesium|maplibre&time=...&fx=...&along=...` (procedural scene, no Google tiles) and original screenshots `docs/images/holo-preview-*.png`.
- Tests for turn guidance, the route guide, meshes, matrices, the ground grid, the shader sources, the building style, the label clamp, the fx scan band and the Terms guard (771 tests in total, up from 610).

- Day and night follow the device clock while the position is simulated: the demo route is in Berlin, so the real Berlin sun could be bright day under a 22:04 HUD clock. The scene now uses the sun at the route when its local solar time equals the device's time of day (`sunMoment` in `@waylit/core`, tested for night, noon, sunset, time zone independence, the date line and polar cases). A switch on the start screen (and `?sun=clock` or `?sun=real`) picks "Light follows my clock" (default) or "Real sun at the route". The dev time override still wins and real GPS needs one constant changed. The HUD clock still shows the device time.
- Top scrim and readable HUD text: a flat linear gradient behind the top of the screen, stronger by day (driven by the daylight factor), a small dark text shadow on text over the scene, brighter secondary text colours and new `scrim` and `shadow` design tokens. A test checks WCAG contrast (4.5:1 small text, 3:1 large) against bright sky, sunlit ground and night backgrounds.
- Futuristic look for the 3D scene: one full screen Cesium post process (colour only, no depth or picking) with a navy and teal grade, a magenta to cyan split tone at sunset, a cyan Sobel edge glow strongest at night, a horizon haze band, a vignette and a faint scanline, all driven by the daylight state. It steps down from high to low to off when the median frame rate drops below 30 fps, with `?fx=off|low|high` overrides and the level shown in the dev overlay. The MapLibre fallback gets the same look as a style colour grade. With the effect on, night tiles are also darker with a faint warm street light lift. In Google mode the effect is off by default until the maintainer has read the Google terms. A development page (`/dev/fx-preview?dev=1`) renders a procedural scene through the real stage.
- Dev overlay shows the 60 second, 5 minute and whole ride median frame rate and the ride time. A Chrome based layout check (`apps/web/scripts/layout-check.mjs`) verifies that nothing covers the credits strip or other HUD parts at five landscape sizes.
- DEPLOYING.md: Cesium ion Community is non-commercial only, the free allowance, token scope and asset limits, two sets of terms, and the EEA note.

- 3D environment on the riding screen behind the provider interface in `@waylit/map-provider`: `CesiumGoogleProvider` (CesiumJS with Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles through Cesium ion, no Google key, never reads heights from the tiles) and `MapLibreFallbackProvider` (OpenFreeMap vector tiles, open terrain, own style). A selector picks CesiumJS when `NEXT_PUBLIC_CESIUM_ION_TOKEN` is set and falls back to MapLibre otherwise, with `?map=fallback|cesium` overrides. Development builds default to the fallback to protect the Cesium ion allowance. A Cesium failure swaps once to the fallback with a quiet amber note and never retries in a loop.
- Third person follow camera driven by the simulated GPS for both providers (rider in the lower middle, horizon in the upper third), lighting that follows the solar daylight state (day, golden hour, night) including the developer time override, and lazy loaded provider chunks that are not part of the start screen.
- Mid range phone performance settings: device pixel ratio cap, CesiumJS screen space error and cache tuning with no globe, base imagery, skybox or widgets, a frame cap (30 fps riding, 10 fps idle), pause when the tab is hidden or the ride has ended, and a development only overlay with fps, 60 second median, 1 percent low and tile statistics.
- Credits strip content: Google logo and data credits plus the Cesium ion logo for CesiumJS, OpenFreeMap, OpenMapTiles and OpenStreetMap contributors for the fallback, never covered by the HUD.
- Dependencies: `@cesium/engine` 26.5.0, `@cesium/core` 0.1.1 and `maplibre-gl` 6.19.0, pinned, with the lockfile. CesiumJS and MapLibre static files are copied into `apps/web/public` by a build step.
- Tests for the pure map logic: provider selection, follow camera, lighting, frame statistics, camera mapping, error classification, the fallback style and the scene controller.
- Project foundations: Apache 2.0 licence, README, contribution guide, code of conduct, security policy, issue and pull request templates, CI, Dependabot configuration and project documentation.
- Project structure: pnpm workspace with a Next.js App Router web app (`apps/web`) and shared packages `core`, `vehicle`, `map-provider` and `ui-tokens`, with strict TypeScript, ESLint, Prettier and Vitest. Pinned Node.js 22 and pnpm with a committed lockfile.
- Design system: dark glass tokens with electric blue and cyan accents, exposed as TypeScript values, CSS variables and a Tailwind theme. Documented in `docs/DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`.
- Placeholder home page with the app name, a safety notice and a link to the source code. Runs with no keys and makes no network calls.
- Read-only vehicle interface and map provider interface types.
- Docs: `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, run-locally steps in the README and `docs/DEPLOYING.md`, a proposed self-host path (static export), and dependency licences in `docs/THIRD_PARTY.md`.
- Simulation layer: a simulated GPS in `@waylit/core` that follows an original demo route with smoothing, playback speed, pause and resume and an injected clock, a mock Segway Ninebot MAX G3 telemetry adapter in `@waylit/vehicle` (battery drain, range, trip distance and duration, 25 km/h default limit, vehicle speed separate from GPS speed) with a GPS fallback selector, and a mock media player model with invented tracks and generated artwork, wired to the browser Media Session API with a Web Audio tone engine and a `useMockMedia` hook in `apps/web`. No new dependencies.
- Optional `NEXT_PUBLIC_WAYLIT_SOURCE_URL` so forks can point the source link at their own repository.
- PWA shell: web app manifest (standalone, landscape, design token colours), original app icons generated by a dependency free script, a hand written service worker with versioned caches and an offline page, an install button and fallback hint on the start screen, an update prompt, safe area handling, a rotate prompt in portrait on the ride screen, an optional landscape lock after a tap in the installed app, a Screen Wake Lock hook, and a dismissible first run safety notice. A placeholder `/ride` screen shows the full screen shell and wake lock. Documented in `docs/PWA.md`, including how to test on Android Chrome over USB and the limits of a PWA.

- Riding screen (`/ride`), a floating HUD over a scene that fills the whole screen: a bare, very large vehicle speed top left with GPS speed under it, the next turn arrow, distance, street instruction, time and distance left and arrival beside it. Local time, mock temperature, a connectivity dot and a round plain SVG minimap (route line, position dot and a clearly visible heading cone, no map tiles) at top right. One joined glass dock along the bottom: the media player (wired to the shared `MediaPlayer` and the Media Session API) on the left, the vehicle dashboard (battery, range, trip distance and time) and End trip on the right. The centre is a dark gradient kept free for the 3D scene, and a reserved, darker credits strip below the dock that nothing overlaps. Above 8 km/h the media controls and End trip need a short hold. Dock panels are more solid in daylight and more see-through at night.

- `enterFullscreen` and `exitFullscreen` in `apps/web/src/pwa/fullscreen.ts`: feature detected, silent on refusal, and skipped when the app is already fullscreen through its manifest.

- Solar module in `@waylit/core` (NOAA solar position algorithm, original code, no dependencies): `getSolarState` for sun elevation, azimuth and phase (day, golden hour, civil and nautical twilight, night), `getSunTimes` for solar noon, sunrise, sunset and civil and nautical dawn and dusk with polar day and polar night handled, and `daylightFactor`. Independent of the machine time zone. Tested against reference values for five places, both solstices, the equator and the polar cases.
- Dev time override for the riding screen (development builds and `?dev=1`): a Time row with Live, Dawn, Noon, Sunset and Night presets, a time of day slider and a date field, and `?time=` in the address, so day, sunset and night can be shown on demand (success criterion 5). The clock and arrival time follow the override.
- `useDaylight()` and `DaylightProvider` expose the sun, phase, a smooth daylight factor and a warmth value for the 3D lighting task. New screenshots at dawn, noon, sunset and night in `docs/images`.
- Read only Bluetooth adapter for the Segway Ninebot MAX G3 (Amendment 8), in `@waylit/vehicle`. It is opt in and the mock stays the default. A frame layer written from documented protocol facts (frames, fragmentation and reassembly, AES-128 through WebCrypto, a four byte tag and counter, the three step handshake), a `WebBluetoothAdapter` with tap to connect, a name prefix and one service allowlist, queued GATT operations, disconnect handling with backoff and a fall back to GPS speed, and the states idle, connecting, authenticating, connected, auth-needed, disconnected, unsupported and error. If this phone is not paired it says "auth needed" and shows no data. Pairing writes a secret to the scooter, so it only happens when the rider taps Pair this phone and reads a plain warning first. Unit and scale choices are provisional and unverified on a real scooter, see `docs/VEHICLE.md`.
- Read only guard: only the three handshake requests and reads of nine allowlisted registers can be encoded or sent. Tests walk every command byte and register number, and a static test scans the source for write and control command names and for any characteristic other than the write and notify ones.
- Vehicle section in the settings sheet: Mock (default) or MAX G3 (Bluetooth), remembered in localStorage with `?vehicle=mock|g3` winning, a Connect button that runs from the tap, a quiet state line (amber for needs attention, red only for a real error), and plain notes ("Reads only. Waylit never sends control commands.", keep the screen on, close the official Segway app, pairing is unverified). Connecting is off above 8 km/h and unsupported browsers (iPhone and iPad, insecure pages, no Web Bluetooth) get a plain message.
- Desk simulator at `?vehicle=g3sim`: a generated fake Bluetooth link with a pretend scooter that emits plausible telemetry frames through the real parser and adapter, so the whole path can be tried at a desk with no hardware.
- `docs/VEHICLE.md` (what is known and unknown, risks, testing with the simulator, nRF Connect and a real G3, safety and the Victorian rules), a vehicle protocol reference and licence rules in `docs/THIRD_PARTY.md`, Bluetooth limits in `docs/PWA.md`. Not affiliated with Segway, names used only to say which vehicle is supported, no logos.

Details are in `docs/DEPLOYING.md`.
- Top HUD without plates (2026-11-26), after the maintainer did not like the dark background behind the top two panels. The top left speed and turn text and the top right time, temperature and dot lose their dark plate, fill and border and sit bare on the scene. A shadow that follows the glyphs keeps them readable: `.wl-hud-shadow` (a `text-shadow`) and `.wl-hud-icon` (a `drop-shadow` filter on the turn arrow, the offline note icon and the connectivity dot), from the new `shadow.scene` and `shadow.sceneIcon` tokens: a 1 pixel dark edge on four sides at 0.72 opacity plus one soft 8 pixel halo at 0.55. No fill, gradient, box shadow or backdrop filter. Sizes, spacing, tap targets and the hold to press behaviour are unchanged, and the old divider is now a 1 pixel spacer. The map settings button, the minimap, the dock, the credits strip, the start caption, the map note and the dev overlay are unchanged.
- Readability of that shadow is modelled, not assumed. A soft halo alone (a 1 pixel close shadow plus an 8 pixel halo at 0.65 and 0.55) measured only 1.0:1 to 1.7:1 at the glyph edge over pure white, well under AA, so the 1 pixel edge was added (the least intrusive fix: at most a 1 pixel stroke, drawn as four hard shadow offsets). Modelled at the glyph edge with the final shadow, the weakest text per colour group is, over pure white, 8.7:1 primary (speed digits 10.1:1), 6.9:1 secondary, 5.0:1 amber and 5.4:1 for the cyan arrow; over a light sky (`#bcd9ee`) 10.4, 8.2, 6.0 and 6.4; over the night horizon 17.9, 14.3, 10.4 and 10.6. Small text needs 4.5:1 and the speed and turn distance 3:1. This is a model, not a measurement: a real sunlit Samsung Galaxy A35 screen has not been checked.
- The guard test `no-screen-darkening.test.ts` still bans any scrim, screen wide gradient, full screen fill and vignette, and now allows a documented local glyph shadow capped at a 12 pixel blur, a 2 pixel offset and 0.9 opacity (a test proves it fails above the cap). It also fails on a plate, fill or border on the navigation and status components and on any `backdrop-filter` or box shadow on the HUD. `readability.test.ts` models the shadow over white, light sky, pale ground and dark scenes for the text shadow and the icon filter, and the layout check measures bare glyphs with the computed shadow over pure white, a light sky and pure black, and fails if the navigation or status group paints a fill, border, box shadow or backdrop filter. The model for the layout check lives in `apps/web/scripts/halo-model.mjs` and `halo-model.test.ts` keeps it in step with `readability.ts`. The layout check also takes `CDP_PORT` for a free debugging port.
- Re-shot the seven `docs/images/hud-preview-*.png` screenshots from the procedural preview (`/dev/holo-preview?dev=1&hud=1`, no Google tiles and no network). The `ride-*.png` and `map-settings-sheet-915x412.png` screenshots still show the old plates and are not yet re-shot.
- HUD redesign (2026-11-26), after the maintainer found the daylight scrim jarring. The scene now fills the screen with nothing darkening it, and the HUD is a few small, quiet pieces of dark glass. Top left, the speed and the next turn share one plate sized to the text: the speed is smaller (`speed` is now `clamp(3rem, 20dvh, 6.5rem)`, digits about 15 percent of the screen height, down from about 30), with `km/h` and the GPS speed under it, and beside it the turn arrow (cyan when the turn is imminent), the distance (`hudTurn` is now `clamp(1.25rem, 8dvh, 2.5rem)`), the street and one quiet line with the time and distance left and the arrival. Top right, a status chip holds the time, temperature and connectivity dot, and the map settings button sits on its own plate beside a slightly smaller minimap. The dock is a little shorter. The map settings sheet is only as tall as its content. The plates and the dock settle in with a short fade (off with reduced motion). Hold to press, the speed guard, the joined centred dock, fullscreen, the credits strip, the beacon label clamp and the Google tiles rules are unchanged. There is no road speed limit data in milestone 1, so no speed limit sign was added; it is a later item for when speed zone data exists.
- With the visual effect on, the night tint of photographic tiles dims them to about a third and cools them instead of crushing them toward black (from 0.09 to 0.19 up to 0.3 to 0.5).
- Model policy: this redesign was built with Opus 5.5 at the maintainer's explicit request, as a one-off exception to Amendment 2 (Sonnet 5.5 for all work except the critic). The brief is unchanged.

### Removed

- The daylight driven top scrim (`.wl-scrim`, a dark gradient over the top 60 percent of the screen) and its `scrim` tokens. Text over the scene now sits on small plates (`.wl-plate`, new `plate` tokens, 0.74 at night to 0.84 by day) with a two pixel text edge (`shadow.text` is tighter), and passes WCAG AA over pure white: the lowest ratios are 8.2:1 for primary text, 6.5:1 for secondary text, 4.7:1 for the amber note and 4.8:1 for cyan.
- The vignette and the horizon haze band from the scene's post process (`vignette`, `hazeStrength`, `hazeColour` and `hazeWidth` in `FxParameters`, and the `u_haze` uniform), so the corners and the middle of the picture stay as bright as the scene. The colour grade, edge glow, scan band and grid are unchanged.

- Hologram review fixes (task 9): the Terms guard test now covers `tile-shader.ts` and the tile event, any `.pick(`, `czm_globeDepthTexture`, `czm_readDepth` and `tileVisible`, and every `ClassificationType` must be `CESIUM_3D_TILE`. A new `LoadGovernor` steps the hologram down on a slow phone whatever the fx level is (fewer chevrons, then no rails volume), after the screen effect when that is on, and `FxGovernor` and `HoloGovernor` now share a `StepDownGovernor` base. `cesium-holo.ts` reuses its scratch objects each frame. The turn beacon counts from the ride session's distance along the route, the same input as the HUD, and the HUD and beacon agree on the departure (HUD shows it at the very start, the beacon never does). The beacon label no longer rises above 28 percent of the scene. Turn guidance tests assert the label sequence never rises and the exact end of route result.
- The fx post process gained a scan band (both variants) and a ground grid (`high`), driven by new uniforms `u_holo` and `u_motion` and new `FxParameters` fields `scanHalfWidth`, `scanStrength` and `gridStrength`. The scene controller now passes the route to the provider and calls `setGuidance` each frame.
- The first run safety notice sits above the credits strip and the 3D scene starts only after it is acknowledged, so the Google logo and credits are never covered.
- The ground height of the demo route is 85 m above the ellipsoid (about 45 m above sea level at Tempelhof plus a geoid undulation of about 40 m at Berlin), down from 95 m and then 90 m after the task 8 reviews. A documented estimate and still a constant, never read or derived from tiles.

The MapLibre fallback is unchanged.
- DEPLOYING.md now says where Google's road safety requirements are linked (the Routes API and Route Optimization API terms, and the general terms for the EEA), instead of crediting the Map Tiles policies page, and no longer says the Google pages could not be fetched.

- Service worker version bumped to v2. It now also leaves `/cesium/` and `/maplibre/` alone, and tests cover the Cesium ion, Google, OpenFreeMap and Mapterhorn hosts. Docs, `.env.example` and the credits strip height were updated for the 3D scene. The unused `NEXT_PUBLIC_GOOGLE_MAPS_API_KEY` variable was removed.

The manifest now asks for `display: "fullscreen"` with `display_override` of `fullscreen` then `standalone`, and the riding screen requests the Fullscreen API from the Start trip tap and leaves it when the trip ends, so the phone's status bar and navigation bar are hidden while riding and the app draws its own top strip. - The dev controls are now always one row tall (the Time button swaps the simulation row for the time page, and going back keeps the override), so with `?dev=1` the Start trip button, the arrival line and the dock no longer overlap at 915 by 412, 740 by 360, 667 by 375, 800 by 360 or 1280 by 720.
- The dock gives the media half more room at small widths (a smaller artwork tile, tighter gaps and padding, and the same inset as the HUD above it), so a title such as "Low Battery Waltz" shows in full at 667 by 375 and a longer one ends in a clean ellipsis. Controls stay at least 44 px.
- A scooter whose first sample arrives after GPS has already moved a few metres is no longer reported as a "mixed" trip. The first 50 m counted before the scooter's first sample are still added to the trip but do not count as GPS use; a scooter that never reports still reads as GPS, and any dropout afterwards still counts.
- The dock no longer squeezes the track title at 667 by 375: the vehicle half is sized to its content, the title fits and a long artist line ends in an ellipsis with all controls fully visible.
- The dev buttons are 44 px instead of 32 px.
- Trip distance and time no longer lose the stale window when scooter telemetry drops out. `TripTracker` remembers where GPS and the ride clock were at the last new vehicle sample and backfills from GPS when telemetry goes stale or returns.
- The trip summary note says whether distance came from GPS, from a mix of the scooter and GPS, or only from the scooter, using the whole trip instead of the last source.
- The minimap heading cone was nearly invisible. It is now a wedge with a gradient fill and an outline.
